Foreword
A working tool, not a rulebook.
SEARULEA is building the infrastructure of Moroccan aquaculture — an integrated value chain at Ras El Ma, Oriental Region, designed to operate as a single system. This document defines how the brand looks, speaks and behaves. It is a working tool for everyone who designs, writes or communicates on behalf of SEARULEA — to ensure consistency, precision and impact in every communication.
Strategic Foundation
Sections 1.1 — 1.8
Built to structure an industry.
1.1
Our Mission
SEARULEA builds Morocco's capacity to produce, transform and scale aquaculture on its own terms —
an integrated value chain designed to meet international standards from day one.
1.2
Our Vision
To make Ras El Ma the reference point of Mediterranean aquaculture — a model that the wider North African industry will look to replicate: integrated, traceable, biosafe and sovereign.
1.3
Core Values
Four commitments, engineered into the CAPEX — never added later.
I
Rigour
Precision without excess. Discipline, operational control and technical standards under every decision.
II
Integration
Offshore farming, fresh processing, refrigerated export and traceability — one stage supports the next.
III
Sovereignty
The capacity to produce, transform and create value locally, on Morocco's own terms.
IV
Sustainability
Biosafety, solar energy and social commitments built into the structure — never an afterthought.
1.4
Our Story
Morocco has 3,500 kilometres of coastline and direct access to European markets. Yet juveniles are imported, feed is sourced abroad, and foreign operators structure an industry Morocco is naturally positioned to lead. SEARULEA was founded by Abdellah Mansouri to change that. Structurally. In Ras El Ma, on a 375-hectare offshore government concession, an integrated aquaculture value chain now operates as a single system.
1.5
Purpose
SEARULEA exists to build the infrastructure of Moroccan aquaculture — not only to produce fish. Over time, hatchery and feed operations are designed to support the wider development of aquaculture across North Africa. What is built in the Oriental Region today will be observed across the region tomorrow.

Target Audience

1.7
Logo Usage — the lockup system
Four sanctioned forms, one decision rule: the space chooses the lockup, never taste. Note the detail that makes the system: the wordmark's final A is the bare triangle apex — the monogram already lives inside the logotype. Never draw a second crossbar A, and never place the monogram beside the wordmark in any arrangement other than the two lockups below.
Default — use first
Horizontal lockup
Monogram + logotype side by side. Headers, letterheads, signage, footers — any space wider than 3:1 and at least 32 px / 24 mm wide.
Centred compositions
Stacked lockup
Monogram above logotype — the business-card composition. Covers, packaging fronts, roll-ups, square and portrait spaces.
When the mark is already present
Logotype alone
The wordmark with its apex-A. Card backs, page footers, spines — wherever the monogram already appears nearby, or strips too shallow for the lockup.
Below 32 px / 24 mm
Monogram alone
Avatars, favicons, stamps, embroidery, app icons. The smallest ambassador of the brand — clearspace of one droplet row.

Contrast — approved grounds
Teal on light grounds holds 8.9:1 against Pearl and 7.4:1 against Sea Foam; White on Deep Sea holds 9.8:1. Photography always takes a Deep Sea scrim of at least 40% before the logo lands on it.

Monogram Anatomy
Droplets in stepped rows closing to a single apex. Dot sizes step down by a fixed ratio; spacing is optical, never redrawn.

4.6
Color Values Explanation
HEX / RGB
Screen values — web, decks, social, product UI. Always use the exact values; never eyedrop from photos.
CMYK
Four-colour offset and digital print. Values are calibrated for coated stock; request a proof for uncoated.
PANTONE
Spot-colour print, packaging, signage and fabric dyeing — the reference when colour must not drift.
